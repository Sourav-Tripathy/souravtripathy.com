A Hello from Van Gogh…

Me, Starry Night, and a random June night

June 25, 2025 / 6 min read Read on Medium ↗

It’s the middle of June and the weather is neither too dry nor too wet… it feels polite, but somehow it is heavy on the heart. I don’t know why — is it the occasional drizzle of rain, or the sudden shift from rain to sun? Only my heart knows, and it’s not trying to talk to my brain. So I am clueless…

So, instead of wandering out, or sitting with a book, or putting on a show on Netflix, I just brewed myself a cup of black coffee from the instant coffee bottle and opened my laptop. My home screen: Starry Night by Van Gogh.

I stopped there instead of clicking on Chrome or VS Code. I just sat still while my cup remained halfway to my mouth, the steam rising from the coffee, hazing up my spectacles, and me staring at the screen through the misty glass. I put down the cup… didn’t wipe my spectacles clean, as I felt a weird tingle in my brain.

The home screen has been set to Starry Night for the past eleven months just because I liked the colours — it felt unique to my eyes somehow. But I never cared to stare at it. I know all those things people write about it, describing how this painting was done by Van Gogh when he was in the mental asylum, and this was what he saw from his window… the yellow and blue colours appearing due to his colour blindness… and some say it shows how he manifested the world — bright sun indicating positivity and all that bullshit.

But since I’ve realized that art has no meaning in itself — that it is the consumer who defines what it feels or brings — I never looked for a feeling. And since that feeling never came, I never thought about it.

But today, in the middle of June, on a random Tuesday night, somehow, I feel different…

I am feeling a bit of anguish in me, while feeling calm by looking at the picture. I don’t know why… the fluidity of the painting and the messy brushstrokes symbolize for me the fragility of everything in existence. The utter ephemerality is brought out and laid before me… and I don’t flinch. I see the black lines posing as a tree and imagine myself being one of them — staring at the sun and moon simultaneously, as if I am the creator of them, consuming them at the same time.

The small sleepy town remains unaware of the absurdity above, sleeping in silence under the billion-dollar show put on by the stars.

“I need some liquid,” the tree says, and my hand automatically reaches for the coffee, which is only a little warm now.

I realize the haze is no longer over my spectacles. It has vanished. I sip three times, continuously… put the cup down… and suddenly, I realize — oh, there is a church in the picture. Standing tall.

I drift into thought… was it intentional? Did the asylum Van Gogh stayed at have windows that overlooked a church? Because at that time, people were deeply religious and believed surrendering to God could somehow bring sanity… when it’s just a momentary illusion. People stay in because they fear to question.

And I find myself asking more questions… did Van Gogh believe in God? Or did he just paint it because he saw it? Or because he thought: If I am painting houses, I ought to put a church — because people need it… because morality mostly comes from there and not from within?

I get perplexed… and somehow amused.

Maybe that’s what this night is about — not answers, just questions.
Questions that know how to sit beside you like an old friend… not the loud kind, but the one who knows you well enough to not interrupt your silence.

The painting hasn’t changed, and yet tonight it feels like it’s breathing with me. Maybe it’s always been like this, and I never had the right kind of silence to notice…or maybe I was just too loud inside to listen.

I move the mouse a bit, not to open anything — just to see the tiny arrow move across the stars like a comet wandering aimlessly… and I think of how easy it is to exist like that… floating around in someone else’s masterpiece, pretending to mean something.

The coffee’s cold now.

I don’t drink it. I just hold the mug, as if the warmth left behind could still seep into me if I waited long enough…

The window beside me is slightly open, and the curtain flutters a little… that same polite wind, the same polite weather… pretending to be light while it weighs something on your chest that you can’t name.

I stare again at the black silhouette in the painting — the tree, or whatever it is. People say it’s a cypress, associated with mourning and death. Of course it is. Van Gogh wouldn’t just paint a pretty sky without a reminder of how close beauty and decay walk together…

And somehow, I feel like I am that tree tonight, stretching upwards without knowing why. Maybe hoping that if I stretch long enough, I will touch something… God, purpose, or just a better version of me.

I laugh quietly — not the happy kind, more like a tired exhale that forgets it was once laughter.

The houses in the town look so asleep… so unaware.

And I wonder — do they dream in paintings?
Do they ever try to run away from the canvas they’re trapped in?

Another sip. It’s bitter now. But I don’t mind. Sometimes bitterness is easier to sit with than sweetness — it demands less pretending.

And then something strikes me…

The stars in Starry Night aren’t small. They’re absurdly large, swirling, alive… Maybe Van Gogh didn’t paint what he saw — maybe he painted how it felt to be under the sky when your mind was louder than the world… when the stars refused to be dots and instead became voices… whispers… or screams…

I suddenly realize I’ve been here for hours.

The clock says something irrelevant like 2:41 am… but time doesn’t matter right now. What matters is the quiet between thoughts… the strange peace of confusion…the freedom of not chasing meaning but simply sitting in its absence like it’s okay to be lost.

I close the laptop.

The stars disappear… but they’ve already sunk somewhere inside me.

And tomorrow, when the sun rises and people start talking about plans and tasks and the weather, I’ll still be carrying this night quietly with me…
like a painting I didn’t know how much I needed to look at… until I did.

I switch off the lamp and lie down on my bed.

I hear a dog barking outside. I go near the window and see the dog on the side road, staring straight and barking. I just smile and return to my bed…
