Between Sips

The existential dread of instant Coffee

January 26, 2026 / 5 min read Read on Medium ↗

The dread comes later. It never visits you when you first start.It waits until your hands have memorised the ritual. The scoop. The kettle’s scream. The pour. Only then does it slip into the kitchen with the steam and sit with you.

You see, there are two ways to make it. Two paths to the same quiet end. You can be the kind of person who boils it all together. Water and powder in a small pot. A chaotic birth. Stirring the dark swirl until it foams up, threatening to spill over. A method that feels stubborn. Like you are forcing the world to become coffee.Or you can be like me. You can know the truth that sits right there on the label if you bother to read it. This powder in the jar. This fine brown dust. It was once real coffee. They brewed it. Then they froze it dry. They took a living thing and turned it into a ghost. A memory in granular form. So you put two spoons of this ghost into a cup. You pour hot water over the dead. And you watch. You watch the tiny universe. The frantic bubbles rushing to the surface. A caffeinated big bang as the water turns weary brown ,then a deep, resigned black. No residue,no evidence. It just dissolves like it was never really there.

You drink it black. You have to. Sugar would be a lie too sweet to bear. You drink it with a certain indignation. And you feel it then. The lack. The hollow space where life should be. It just feels like nothing. A bitter nothing.

I loved it once. I swear I did. At the start, its bitterness was a language I was proud to learn. It tasted like being grown up. It was a sharp, clear signal in the morning fog. My whole family are tea people. Proper tea. Ginger crushed against the side of the pot. A crack of black pepper. Milk simmered just right. A taste that wraps around your bones. Coffee was my own quiet rebellion.

Then came the day. I was in a coffee place. A proper one. It smelled of earth and smoke. I looked at the board and the words felt solid. Espresso. I asked for a double shot. The barista looked at me. He actually stopped. He wiped his hands on a cloth, a slow movement. He said, “A double shot. You are sure.” It wasn’t really a question. I nodded. And he said, softer, “In all my time here, no one has ever ordered that.” He wasn’t being rude. It was just a fact. A strange little fact in a country where tea is the default setting of the soul.

He made it. I watched the machine hiss. It came in a tiny, thick cup. A dark pool with a creamy skin, flecked with brown. It looked serious. The first sip was a bolt of pure, dark joy almost violent in its clarity.

It was bitter, yes. But it was a bitter that opened doors. It had a bright, sharp edge. A citrusy spark that made my mouth wake up in places I didn’t know were asleep.This was a story of a bean, and a hill, and a roast. It was alive. Terribly, beautifully alive.

Now I am a man split in two.

Out there, I am an espresso hunter. But here. At home. In my own kitchen. I live with the ghost. I do not own a machine. I have no french press. My skill ends at the kettle. So I face the cupboard. I reach for the jar. The one with the granules. The resignation is a quiet taste in my mouth, even before I make it.

This is the coffee of reality. Of Monday mornings. Of rent and routines and the long, flat hours between inspirations. It is the coffee of compromise.

I am drinking it right now. As I write these words. The cup is warm against my palm. I hate its lifelessness. I hate the flat, stale echo of a taste it presents. But I also understand it. There is a perverse comfort in its emptiness. It asks nothing of me. It offers no revelations. It just is. A steady, mute hum in the bloodstream and because of the dread, I have realised, this feeling is not really about the coffee.

This dread is about seeing the pattern. The instant coffee is a perfect copy of so much else. The conversations that have become routines. The joys remembered in shorthand. The dreams freeze-dried into safe, manageable goals. We reconstitute them every day. We add the hot water of our effort and watch the familiar bubbles rise. We drink it down and call it living.

The espresso is the rare. The instant is the daily. The one that lets you function.And in the space between sips. In the quiet of my kitchen with this cup of ghost in my hand ,I wonder with an empty face.

All these thoughts fades slowly. Like the last of the heat from this cup. I will finish it. And tomorrow. I will probably do it all over again.
