The Homicide of I

Some late night thoughts wrapped in crisis

November 22, 2025 / 4 min read Read on Medium ↗

It hurts me.

It hurts to the core of my heart, every night when I sit down in my chair and stare at the empty black screen with my hands on the keyboard, hoping I might let out all this pain but the pain seems not to care. This ache that ails me has no name, because I who suffer from it am anonymous to it. Its origin and end are a mystery, and if the origin is unknown, imagine how deep its reason runs.

Every night this repeats, and for some moments of soothing I gulp down a cup of black bitterness without sugar, hoping it might burn out some of that ache. I await an answer, and all I get is a silent abyss telling me: either sleep or stare. I get defeated every day after hours of fight, and the next day I re-appear to fight again, already knowing the result. 
Why?
I ask myself, and I receive no answer … because the pain has hollowed me out. I no longer care, I no longer wish, I no longer crave for human connection, even though the tears rolling down my eyes tell a different story, and both my glasses and the hand that wipes them are the witnesses. But even that hand, which knows, rests quietly on the keyboard without pressing any keys.

It’s been seventeen days already. I am defeated again. I will rest again. But for some reason the green walls in my bedroom seem gentler now. Maybe I desire a bit more sleep or perhaps no sun at all.

Sometimes I feel this pain isn’t even mine anymore as if it has taken a life of its own and I’m just the host it feeds on. The more I try to name it, the more it slips into another shape. That’s the problem with understanding: once you begin to grasp it, it becomes something else.

I think I’ve grown comfortable with this discomfort. There’s a strange peace in knowing the ache will return and it’s the only thing that does without fail. People talk about moving on, healing, acceptance … words that sound like furniture in a room you never enter. I don’t think I want to heal anymore. I just want to stop being surprised by how heavy existence feels at night.

The walls hum in quiet colors now. Maybe that’s mercy , things that exist without needing to understand you. I’ve started to believe pain doesn’t need to end to be bearable; it only needs to become familiar. And I, somehow, have become familiar with my own undoing.

There was a time I thought this would pass …a few nights of sleep, a walk, a voice. But it isn’t fatigue. It’s erosion. Not the violent kind, but the slow one that teaches you how to disappear without leaving. Maybe I didn’t kill myself; maybe I just stopped feeding what made me human. You can live a long time that way …almost alive, almost gone.

Every night, the screen reflects a man I barely know. His eyes have rehearsed stillness. His hands hover, pretending they could still write. Words used to be rescue boats. Now they’re driftwood , splinters I cling to because drowning feels worse.

I think about the version of me that once believed in meaning. He fought the silence. I envy him not for being happy, but for having someone left inside him to lose.

And maybe that’s the quiet tragedy that I’m still here to narrate my own absence. The homicide was never final. It’s ongoing ,patient and methodical. Every breath testifies against me, and I confess willingly, because guilt is the last proof that something once existed.

So I keep sitting before this black screen, night after night, recording the slow murder of what used to be me …waiting for the last thought to fade, for the hands to stop hovering, for the silence to finally finish what I could not.

Someday I will stop writing,and might just disappear and existence will have to find another entity to fit into my name if it wishes…
