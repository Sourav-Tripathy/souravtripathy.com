The Arithmetic of Theoretical Mornings

Coffee, Universes, and Almosts

March 22, 2025 / 4 min read Read on Substack ↗

I wake up half-expecting sunlight, the warm, disheveled kind that drips into March mornings without much conviction. But when I pull the curtain aside, the sky is just… dull. Thick with unsure clouds, heavy with the idea of rain but not the certainty of it. 63 percent chance of rain, I mutter to myself, as if I’m a weather app.

The wind sneaks in through the window I forgot to latch last night: chilly, damp, carrying the faint scent of rain and a weird fruity smell of dried marigold, leaving me perplexed…

August weather in March. The kind of sky that makes you say "it might rain" even when you know it won’t.

I drag myself to the kitchen, not really walking, just floating through the motions. I fill the kettle, click the switch. The hum of boiling water is too loud. My hand reaches for the jar of instant Arabica, fingers clumsy, knocking over a spoon. It clatters. I don’t pick it up. Three scoops into the mug. More than usual. I want the bitterness today; the kind that sits heavy on the tongue. I notice the mug has a brown stain at the rim, blue outside, white inside. I used to like how it looked. I still do, maybe because it reminds me of the ocean.

I pour the water in a slow, careless stream, watching the coffee bloom unevenly. Bubbles form and burst.

Tiny universes collapsing.

I don’t stir. Just watch. Then the first sip: sharp, earthy, wrong. The bitterness spreads thickly over my tongue. Stronger than I expected. I should’ve brewed fresh beans. Should’ve let the water drip slowly through the grounds. Let the coffee unfold itself instead of collapsing instantly. But I didn’t. And now I’m here, with this flat, unconvincing bitterness.

On the way back to my desk, I feel the heaviness of a treason. I glance at the mug and resent it. Instant coffee. What a lazy compromise. Should’ve ground beans.But I didn’t. I sit down. The chair wobbles slightly, and I let it.

The books on the table: Dostoevsky’s guilt, Camus’ absurdity, Deutsch’s infinite threads, Murakami’s talking cats - feel like homework. I’m not in the mood for epiphanies. The pen sits untouched. I should read, but I just stare into the mug instead.

The inside is already stained, a faint brown ring clinging stubbornly to the ceramic. I take another sip, and it tastes stronger, harsher, like the bitterness grew thicker while I wasn’t looking. I keep staring at the stain, and somehow, it feels important. Like a tiny metaphor for something I can’t name. I tilt the mug slightly, watching the brown traces drift upward and downward, marking the edges. Coffee residue and gravity, that’s all it is. But I keep looking.

Thoughts start spilling in. The wandering kind . What if I had stayed? Or left sooner? Or called back? Or hadn’t? Tiny decisions with enormous shadows.My mind drifts into theoretical math, calculating lives I’ll never live. The sum of wrong turns, the remainders of hesitation, the fractions of almosts. It feels pointless.

Should I let go of these thoughts or indulge them, I ask myself.

My phone chimes.

I reach for it sluggishly, expecting nothing.

Zomato.

"Sourav, Biryani for Saturday?"

And just like that, the entire calculus of my morning dissolved. The coffee, the clouds, the existential drift - they all collapse into the simplicity of biryani. The saffron rice, the heavy warmth of ghee, tender meat falling off the bones, and the tangy raita cooling the edges. I can taste it already. I glance at the books once more, but they have lost all relevance. The coffee too. I sip it again, but it’s tasteless now. The bitterness is just background noise.

I tap on the notification.

One disrupted Saturday morning.
