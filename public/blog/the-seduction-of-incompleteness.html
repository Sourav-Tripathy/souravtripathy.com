The Seduction of Incompleteness

Finding Beauty in What Refuses to Be Solved

July 19, 2025 / 4 min read Read on Substack ↗

I wrote this line nearly a year ago, sitting at home after finishing my engineering degree. I had no job, no defined direction - just the weight of transition and the surreal lightness that comes when you no longer have a structure holding you. The world was suspended in static. I wasn't yet building models. I was one.

Back then, everything felt ungraspable. Not wrong…just floating. Now, nearly a year into learning machine learning , that line hasn’t aged. If anything, it grew roots.
Because the more I push into it, the clearer one thing becomes: reality is a construct so cleverly woven, even its deceptions feel like certainties.

We like our truths sealed.
A proof is admired not just for being right, but for being done.
A model is “trained” when the loss plateaus.
A paper gets published not because it found the chaos, but because it tamed it.

And yet…I’ve seen the models. I’ve watched them converge.
They predict, they cluster, they translate. But every time you push them past the training distribution, they blink like a deer in headlights.
They hallucinate.
They guess with high confidence.
They create with no grounding.

That’s not failure. That’s revelation.
They're telling us something fundamental: we don’t understand the world…we scaffold it.

We don’t learn truth. We learn correlations.
And we do it because it's the only way to stay sane in an uncomputable universe.

Richard Feynman, in Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman!, never tried to be a genius. He just refused to inherit assumptions.
Whether cracking safes at Los Alamos or rebuilding calculus from scratch as a teenager, his guiding principle was simple: "What do I know, and how do I know it?"

There’s a moment I keep returning to.
He’s sitting in Brazil, watching students memorize physics without understanding any of it. He gets up and tears the whole thing apart…not to be dramatic, but because he couldn’t stand that facade of knowledge. The structure without soul.

In another moment, he reflects on seeing a flower ; others admire its color, and he says: "I can see much more than they, for I understand the cells, the pigments, the biology... it only adds to the beauty."
That hit hard.
Because that's the thing…they say understanding ruins magic. But Feynman says : real understanding is the magic.

David Deutsch, in The Beginning of Infinity, takes this even further.
He tells us that all progress - scientific, moral, philosophical : is rooted in the idea that explanations can always be improved.

There is no final theory. No perfect model. No end to understanding.
Only beginnings and that to infinitely many of them.

He writes:

“Problems are inevitable. But problems are solvable.”

That’s the paradox.
You never arrive. You only find better paths.
And those paths? They begin at the exact point most people stop.
At the first sign of a loose thread.

To me it feels like , he wants us to declare war on finality.
And while learning ML, I feel that same hum beneath everything.
Every model I build isn’t a solution…it’s a story we’re telling ourselves about a data distribution that will change tomorrow.

And we still do it.
Because the alternative - silence , is worse.

We admire theories that close, systems that converge, lives that settle.
But the truth is ,Reality, in its deepest laws, refuses to obey the language of common sense.

Even in mathematics, Gödel slipped in and whispered:
"You can’t prove everything that's true. Not from within the system."
Even perfection can be incomplete.

And yet, we crave this incompleteness.
We poke at the edges.
We build models not to finish the story, but to interrupt it.

We debug, not because we believe in bug-free code, but because we’re addicted to chasing the thing that doesn’t quite fit.Because there’s something sacred about that chase.Something deeply human about trying to model the unmodelable.
To touch what refuses to be touched.

So I keep that line pinned.
Not for nostalgia. Not for drama.
But as a marker.

A reminder that this tension, this ambiguity, this dance at the edge of comprehension , is where the real work begins.

Where models fail, understanding begins.
Where the equation stops, curiosity speaks.
Where certainty collapses, the infinite opens.

And that abyss? It still intoxicates.
Not because it’s dark.
But because it tells me, there's always more to see.
