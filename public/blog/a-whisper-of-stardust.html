A Whisper of Stardust

Conversations at the Edge of the World

July 24, 2025 / 12 min read Read on Medium ↗

The monsoon had arrived, painting nature with lush green and vibrant hues. But I did not care for the dampness that seeped into the city and my bones. I did not see the romance that poets sang of in the raindrops and the thunder. I only felt the warmth that awaited me inside the house. I had everything I needed: a cosy blanket, a steaming cup of coffee, and a book that transported me to another world. Whether it was a mystery by Agatha Christie or a surreal life by Murakami, I was lost in their words and the magic of the monsoon.

This year, I craved a change. The silence that I once cherished had now become oppressive and dull. I longed for a break from the wetness that surrounded me. So, I left my house in a huff, got into my car, and drove aimlessly. I did not have a destination, only a desire to escape. As I drove through the gentle rain, listening to the soothing Bollywood tunes, something shifted in me. I felt a strange affection for the rain, a softening of my heart.

The wiper swept the rain from the windshield in a steady rhythm, while the fog light pierced the misty air. The road through the forest looked like a dream, wrapped in clouds and dusted with frost. I felt a pull to explore this wonderland, so I turned my car onto the hill road that led to the woods. I drove along, swaying to the melodies of Arijit’s song.

The raindrops stopped falling from the sky, leaving the windshield dry and clear. The wipers continued to move, making a grating sound that annoyed my ears. But I did not care to stop them; I was too captivated by the road ahead. I drove on, eager to see what awaited me at the end of the hill.

A ragged cardboard sign greeted me at the hill’s edge. It had a message scrawled in faded ink:

“End of the road, but the journey really begins here.”

“Damn, man! Whoever made that sign, I’ve got to say he was one hell of a reader and writer,” I spoke to myself with a weird fascination in both my eyes and voice.

I parked the car and took out my pocket watch. It may seem odd, but I love these vintage clocks. Wrist watches were not my style; they seemed too cold and bland. There’s a certain beauty and grace to a pocket watch that’s always drawn me in, even if my friends tease me for being old-fashioned. With a press of my thumb, the hatch pops open, and a gentle tick-tock fills the air — like a warm oven chime announcing a feast.

It was 9:26 pm.

The sky was clear and all the cotton balls had gone away, giving the stars and the crescent moon some time to show their brilliance in the night sky. I reached for the bag on the back seat and unscrewed the flask. I poured the hot coffee into my plain white mug, the one my mom gave me for my birthday. It was a simple gift, but it meant a lot to me.

I opened the trunk and took out a smooth lawn chair. I unfolded it and placed it next to the signboard. I wrapped myself in a thin shawl and sat down, gazing at the sky. I decided to spend the whole night there, sipping coffee and watching the stars. It reminded me of the times I used to sit with my father under the night sky. He would point out the constellations and tell me their stories. He taught me how to appreciate the beauty and mystery of nature. He is gone now, but his memory is still with me.

A sudden light appeared in the darkness, coming closer and closer with each blink of my eye. It was a car racing down the slippery hill road at a reckless speed. I jumped from my seat and waved wildly, trying to get the driver to notice me. The car screeched to a halt, sliding and spinning until it smashed into my door with a loud crash. I was gripped by fear and rage, shocked by what had occurred.

I was ready to yell at the driver, but I put the brakes on my legs when I saw a girl coming out of the car. She looked at me with apologetic eyes and said, “Sorry.”

“It’s okay,” I said, losing my confidence with a stutter in my voice. “Maybe you had a bad day.”

I felt an uncanny mix of emotions, not knowing what to assess of the situation.

She didn’t say a word, just stared at me with a blank expression. I felt awkward and quickly returned to my lawn chair. I tried to forget the encounter and focus on the stars, but I couldn’t shake off the feeling that she was watching me.

After what seemed like a suspended moment, she walked over to me and asked, “Do you mind if I join you?”

I looked at her and saw a hint of a smile on her lips.

“Sure, why not?” I said, trying to sound casual. “It’s not every night that you get to share this amazing view with someone,” I said with a soothing yet excited voice.

Chivalry had the best of me, so I got up and poured her some coffee from the flask. I handed her the upper part of the flask, making it an impromptu cup, and offered her my chair. She smiled and settled down, wrapping herself in the shawl. I hopped on the car hood and reclined, feeling a weird mix of thrill and anxiety.

We were silent for a while, gazing at the stars. I wanted to know what was on her mind, but I was too shy to ask.

Then, she spoke. “What brings you here, all by yourself, at this hour? Did you have a breakup or a rough day?”

She sounded inquisitive and also a bit playful. I couldn’t help but grin at such an assumption.

Girlfriend? That was a word I had never used in my life.

“Why are you smiling?” she asked at my shocked reaction.

“You’re talking about something that’s impossible for me,” I said, shaking my head. “I’ve never had a girlfriend, or anything close to that. My life is just my boring 9–5 IT job and a shelf full of books and Netflix shows. I’m not exactly a catch, you know.”

I tried to sound humorous, but I felt a pang of sadness.

She looked at me with a kind expression and said, “Don’t say that. You seem like a nice guy. And you read books, right? Even if you work in an IT firm.”

She imitated my voice, creating a moment of laughter.

A warmth spread in my chest, a new sensation. She teased me softly, making me smile. She eased my nerves, even though we just met. She stirred something in me, a spark of life. It was an odd night, unlike any other. I was shy, but I felt relaxed with her. I was chatting with a stranger, something I seldom did. Maybe it was the pandemic’s isolation, the craving for human touch. That’s what crossed my mind, as I laughed along with her.

“We are both failures, aren’t we? We have no one to fall back on when our own thoughts go absurd,” she spoke with a melancholic voice, as if she had suffered a great loss.

I heard the bitterness in her words, the frustration of not finding a way out.

She glowed like a firefly in the dark, her beauty captivating me. Her hair was long enough to frame her face, which lit up with dimples when she smiled. Her nose was cute and pointy, and her eyes were the biggest I had ever seen. I knew everyone had their own beauty, but she seemed like the ideal embodiment of a woman to me.

The beauty of a living thing is not the atoms that go into it but the way these atoms are put together….

I do not know why but this quote by Carl Sagan kept appearing in my thoughts while I was basking in her beauty under the moonlight.

“I know you are having some weird thoughts about me, probably that I was on a suicide mission here, and be happy that you are right, but the moment has passed and my judgment is back to square one again,” she spoke reluctantly.

“Hey, don’t take this the wrong way, but our lives are not ours to end whenever we want. The people who know us might be affected by our decision, and it is not fair to make them sad when we have never made them happy,” I said in an arrogant tone, but it seemed to have little or no effect on her. She just sat there like a robot on the lawn chair, staring at the sky.

“You seem to be the kind of guy who makes pure decisions, and yet you end up in the same place as me, a complete opposite on many grounds. Isn’t it ironic that no matter what we do or think, we always have this moment once in our lives?” she spoke in a rhetorical tone as if she hated life.

Her way of questioning me made me think that we had a similar outlook on the world. I quickly grasped her way of thinking and replied in a strange but thoughtful voice:

“Yes, and that’s because we are stuck in this world where everything ends badly. But that doesn’t mean we have to choose death, which has no feelings to hurt us. What about the life you haven’t lived, don’t you fear missing it? What if there is a lot of happiness waiting for you in the next moment; you miss it and regret it in the afterlife, if there is one ?”

She seemed offended by my tone and raised her voice a little. “I have given up on that. So, stop lecturing me like some motivational speaker. You have already made me do something I never wanted to do. Now I am here talking to you instead of lying on the ground covered in blood at the end of this hill, waiting to be devoured by crows and eagles. Life does not work out the way you imagine. You are a free person who spends your whole Saturday night under this view, away from worldly problems. I wish I had that kind of life, but instead, I am buried under the mountain of my desires and my duties towards the people I barely know. It is not easy to escape from responsibility and just sit idly, hoping for everything to turn out good. That is nothing but a distant dream for me. You see, I lose myself every day to fuel others’ hopes. It is said that in life one should always focus on one’s own life rather than others, but in reality, that is just a bunch of nonsense that only the successful and rich say.”

She was true to what she felt, openly telling me that life is always unjust, and we just have to make a go of it or become insane.

“What you are saying is true, but shouldn’t you take responsibility for your life? You were not born with these burdens. You made a choice at some point that led you to this situation. The basic principle in life is that you must accept that you are the most important person on this earth. Everything that is happening is a consequence of your actions. When you think like that, you try to come up with creative ideas to improve what you want to see. That’s when the magic happens and you pursue the greatness.” I spoke as if I understood these words perfectly

She got a little exasperated by such answer of mine because I was speaking like a motivational speaker, where she went to change her life but was bored to sleep, but this time it was different I was not talking on a stage but on the point of death and life, on the very junction where magic was bound to happen.

“Why do you think life is beautiful and is more worthy to live?” she questioned me by raising her eyebrow….

“What makes autumn or winter or any season special?” I cross-questioned her in order to make her realise the answer…

“Nothing, just the fact that they exist for a period of time and we never see them again until their time has arrived,” she spoke with a certain appeal.

Exactly!

“You see, everything that is doomed is beautiful, and so is life, and in immortality, beauty finds no definition, and maybe that’s why gods, if they exist, envy us,” I said, reviving a bookish personality.

As I lifted the coffee mug to my lips, hoping for a sip even though it was cold, I realised it was empty. I jumped off the hood to get more, but suddenly everything changed. She was gone, and to my shock, I was sitting on the lawn chair. I felt like I was losing my mind. I looked at my hand and saw no cup. I glanced down and saw my favourite mug broken into pieces on the ground.

I did not feel terrible, strangely enough. The sun was slowly peeking from the horizon, and its amber rays were trying to kiss the earth between the gaps of trees.

Last night had taught me that nothing in this world truly mattered to us. We project our fantasies on everything, making them look as we wish. I loved the mug not for my mother’s gift, but for its white colour, a blank canvas where I could express my soul’s appeal.

I glanced at my pocket watch and saw it had frozen at 9.53. I smiled and left the lawn chair behind, a gift for others like me. I stepped into the hush of my car and drifted down the road with the morning blooming gently behind me.
